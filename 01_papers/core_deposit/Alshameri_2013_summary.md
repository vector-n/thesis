## Summary

This paper characterizes natural bentonite from the Alaslaf-Alqabitah locality in Lahij Province, Yemen, then modifies it two ways — sodium exchange (Na-bentonite) and organic modification with cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB, producing an "organobentonite" or MB = modified bentonite) — and tests both the natural and CTAB-modified forms as adsorbents for ammonium removal from aqueous solution. The paper reports full mineralogical, chemical, and physical characterization of the raw and purified deposit (this is the part most relevant to your thesis), then moves into adsorption kinetics/isotherm modeling (Langmuir, Freundlich, pseudo-first/second-order kinetics) that is not relevant to drilling fluid applications.

## Geological Setting (directly relevant to your Chapter 1 site description)

- **Location:** Alaslaf area, ~7 km northeast of Alrahidah (Alqbitah), Lahij Province, south Yemen. Coordinates given in the paper as 04°29'12" E and 14°79'83" N (as printed — note this coordinate format looks unusual/possibly a typo in the original for standard DMS; worth double-checking against the referenced Khaled & Philippe 2010 source if precise coordinates are needed for your Phase 0 sampling confirmation).
- **Geology:** Region covered by basic rocks (Migmatite–Gneiss) overlain by Triassic volcanics (per Khaled & Philippe 2010), represented by acidic rocks (Rhyolite–Tuff–Ignimbrite), basaltic rocks, and Quaternary deposits.
- **Lithology:** Clay minerals occur as greenish-gray Rhyolitic tuff of Triassic age, decomposed to fine-grained friable soft mud with a "soapy texture" (i.e., bentonite) in its outer rims; some contain pieces of volcanic glass (pitchstone), zeolite, and minor iron oxides. Internal parts of the outcrop remain as fragile-to-semi-coherent rock fragments.
- **Outcrop geometry:** Apparent thickness ranges 2 m (Alaslaf outcrop) to 10 m (Warzan-Simda valley); lateral extent 5–15 m at Alaslaf, up to 70 m at Warzan-Simda valley. Overlain by tuffaceous ignimbrites (1.5 m thick at Alaslaf, up to 15 m at Warzan-Simda).
- A geological map and site photo are included as Figure 1 (a: location map of Yemen showing study area near Ar Rahidah; b: geological map with legend showing basic metavolcanics, paragneiss, amphibolite, basalt, gravel, rhyolite/dacite, ignimbrite/ashflow deposits, gabbro).

## Materials & Methods (relevant for your Chapter 3 methodology comparison)

- **Sample source:** Natural bentonite (NB) from Alaslef-AlQbitah, Lahij province.
- **Purification protocol:** Crushed in mortar → placed in 1-L graduated cylinders → mixed with distilled water → left 12 h → supernatant transferred to other cylinders → few drops 0.1 M HCl added → left 8+ h for bentonite to settle → water discarded → solid precipitate dried at 60°C for 24 h → **process repeated several times**.
- **Na-bentonite preparation:** Solid phase saturated with Na⁺ by stirring in 1 M NaCl solution for 12 h → separated by centrifugation (1,000 rpm/10 min) → repeated 3 times → washed with distilled water to remove excess Cl⁻ (confirmed via AgNO₃ test, i.e., no more chloride precipitate) → dried at 50°C.
- **Organobentonite (MB) preparation:** 10 g bentonite mixed with 100 mL of 2% CTAB solution → mechanical stirring 3 h in water bath at 80°C → filtered, washed with hot distilled water until bromide ions removed (confirmed via 0.1 mol/L AgNO₃) → dried at 60°C for 24 h → finely crushed.
- **Characterization techniques used:** XRD (Philips X'Pert PRO, Cu Kα, 40 kV/40 mA, scan rate 5°/min, 2θ range 0–65°), SEM (Quanta 200), atomic absorption spectroscopy (chemical composition), BET surface area (N₂ adsorption at 77 K, ASAP2020 analyzer), FTIR (NICOLET AVATAR 370 DTGS, 400–4000 cm⁻¹, KBr pellets). CEC, gelling value, and swelling capacity measured per standard method GB/T 20793-2007. Water absorption also per GB/T 20793-2007.

**Note for Chapter 3:** This purification protocol (simple gravity settling + dilute HCl + repeated washing) is notably *simpler* than the thermo-chemical soda-ash activation methods used in Magzoub (2014/2018) or Karaguzel (2010) — it purifies the clay but does **not** perform Na-activation for rheological/drilling purposes; the "Na-bentonite" step here is for a different purpose (creating an ion-exchanged adsorbent precursor, not optimizing drilling rheology). Useful methodological contrast to note in Chapter 3.

### XRD basal spacing (d₀₀₁) — very citable, directly comparable across processing states
- Natural bentonite: **15.3 Å**
- Purified/Na-exchanged bentonite: **12.5–12.6 Å** (paper uses both values in different places — 12.5 in abstract, 12.6 in the results text/figure; likely a rounding inconsistency in the original, worth noting if you cite this precisely)
- CTAB-modified (organobentonite): **19.7 Å**
- Interpretation given: decrease from 15.3→12.5/12.6 Å indicates presence of exchangeable Na⁺ (Na-bentonite formation); increase to 19.7 Å indicates a "pseudotrimolecular layer" configuration of alkyl chains from CTAB intercalation.

### Adsorption performance (NOT relevant to drilling fluid thesis, but noted for completeness)
- Ammonium removal: NB reached ~35% removal, MB reached >78% removal, both at 40 min contact time, pH 6 optimum, 1.5 g optimal dosage of MB (80% removal rate).

- Langmuir R² = 0.9634 (NB), 0.9971 (MB); Freundlich R² = 0.9097 (NB), 0.9304 (MB).
