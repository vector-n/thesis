## Method

**3-factor Box-Behnken Design, 15 runs, Design-Expert 13 (Stat-Ease).** Factors: bentonite (2–20 g), tannin (2–10 g, deflocculant extracted from *Rhizophora* spp. mangrove bark, Kuala Sepetang, Perak), xanthan gum (XG, 0–2 g, viscosifier). Fixed background composition per 350 mL water: 0.25 g soda ash (pH control, maintained at pH 10), 0.25 g caustic soda (water-hardness reduction), 25 g barite (weighting, mud density held at 9.0 ppg). Mixing order and hydration: bentonite + XG mixed into the water/soda-ash/caustic-soda base, barite added, then **24-hour room-temperature prehydration**, then tannin added last (5 min) before rheological testing. All tests at constant 25°C.

**Responses:** PV, AV, YP via Fann-35A dial readings at 3/6/100/200/300/600 rpm, standard API formulas (PV = θ600−θ300; AV = θ600/2; YP = θ300−PV) — **identical response set and formulas to your own planned PV/AV/YP measurements**, though this paper doesn't include gel strength or fluid loss (your thesis has 5 responses vs. this paper's 3).

**Quadratic regression models (Eqs. 4–6, coded units A=bentonite, B=tannin, C=xanthan):**
- PV = 16.00 + 3.12A − 2.88B + 0.5C + 1.25AB − 5.87A² + 2.13B² − 2.13C²
- AV = 45.00 + 10.87A − 6.38B + 3.25C + 1.5AB + 6.25AC + 0.75BC − 14.75A² + 2.75B² − 8.0C²
- YP = 58.00 + 15.50A − 7.0B + 5.50C + 0.5AB + 12.5AC + 1.5BC − 17.75A² + 1.25B² − 11.75C²

## Key results

**Model fit (ANOVA, Table 3):** all three quadratic models statistically significant — PV p=0.0002 (R²=0.9753), AV p<0.0001 (R²=0.9852), YP p=0.0014 (R²=0.9513). Lack-of-fit non-significant in all cases; predicted-vs-actual plots and residual normal-probability plots (Figs. 2–3) support model adequacy.

**⚠️ Citation-integrity flag #1 (internal inconsistency, same as your Falode/Khan and Magzoub flags):** the abstract states AV's R² as **0.9582**, but the body text (Section 3.2) and the ANOVA table itself both give **AV R² = 0.9852**. The body/table figure is almost certainly correct and the abstract contains a typo — **cite 0.9852 for AV**, not the abstract's 0.9582, and note this discrepancy if you ever quote the abstract directly.

**Directional effects:** bentonite increases PV, AV, and YP (linear coefficients: +3.12, +10.87, +15.50 — the largest positive linear coefficient in every model, i.e. **bentonite is the dominant factor here**, unlike the polymer-dominance pattern in Alhajabdalla 2021 and Afolabi 2018 — worth noting as a contrasting precedent for your Chapter 5 discussion of which factor dominates your own BBD). Tannin decreases PV, AV, and YP (linear coefficients −2.88, −6.38, −7.0), attributed to its deflocculant action: providing an alkaline environment that neutralises positive edge charges on clay platelets, causing repulsion rather than flocculation (mechanism cited to Hafshejani et al. 2016). Xanthan gum increases all three responses at low-to-moderate concentration but YP peaks near 64 lb/100ft² before declining at higher XG levels (Fig. 6b) — attributed to XG/clay particle agglomeration at high concentration reducing suspension capacity (cited to Akpan et al. 2020).

**⚠️ Citation-integrity flag #2 (internal contradiction — new, distinct from flag #1):** the **abstract explicitly states "xanthan gum had an insignificant effect on the PV, AV, and YP,"** but the body text (Section 3.3.2) states the opposite — that "the addition of xanthan gum...also significantly affected the rheological properties" and that "the addition of both bentonite and xanthan gum is statistically significant." The Conclusion also states bentonite **and xanthan gum** both increased the rheological properties (implying XG has a real, non-trivial effect), and further calls the bentonite–xanthan gum **interaction** "the most significant" of the study. **The abstract and the body/conclusion directly contradict each other on xanthan gum's significance.** No individual per-term p-value table is available in the extracted text (unlike Alhajabdalla 2021's Table 7) to adjudicate this independently — treat this as an unresolved internal inconsistency in the source paper. If citing XG's significance, hedge appropriately or note the contradiction rather than picking one side silently.

**Multi-response optimisation (Section 3.4, Fig. 7) — this is your Open Thread #4 candidate:** goal set as minimise PV, minimise AV, maximise YP, solved simultaneously (not sequentially) via Design-Expert's numerical optimiser (ramp-block desirability interface, functionally the Derringer & Suich framework even though D&S isn't cited by name in this paper). **Optimum: bentonite = 4.02 g, tannin = 7.29 g, xanthan gum = 0.53 g**, predicting PV = 8.32 cP, AV = 24.41 cP, YP = 32.19 lb/100ft². **Triplicate validation experiments** at these conditions gave PV = 8.67 ± 0.58 cP, AV = 24.33 ± 0.58 cP, YP = 31.33 ± 1.15 lb/100ft² — close agreement with predictions across all three simultaneously-optimised responses.

**This is a genuine 3-response simultaneous desirability optimisation** (opposing objectives: minimise two responses, maximise a third) with independent triplicate experimental validation — a materially stronger multi-response precedent than Alhajabdalla (2021), which only ever optimised one response at a time repeated across scenarios. It doesn't reach your full 5-response case (no gel strength or fluid loss here), but it is the best multi-response worked example in your reference base so far. **Still worth checking Satiyawira (2025)** in case it covers a full 5-response (or closer to 5) simultaneous optimisation.

Use alongside Derringer & Suich (1980) itself as your methodological backbone citation; note it doesn't cover your full 5-response case.
- **Chapter 3/5 — contrasting "which factor dominates" precedent.** Where Alhajabdalla (2021) and Afolabi (2018) both found the polymer/viscosifier term dominating over the particulate/solid term, this paper finds **bentonite (the clay/particulate factor) dominating over the natural deflocculant (tannin)** in all three models. If your own BBD results show clay concentration as the dominant term, this is now a **directly citable precedent for clay-dominance**, complementing rather than conflicting with the polymer-dominance papers (different factor pairings, so not a true contradiction — worth stating explicitly if you cite both patterns).

Minor supporting citation for the "locally-sourced materials can perform a required drilling-fluid function" framing in your introduction, if you want a second example beyond bentonite itself.
- **Chapter 3 — a cautionary methodological note, not a template to copy verbatim:** the paper's own description of its BBD ("16 cubes of factorial points, 4 centre points, and 8 axial points with an alpha of 2") is **internally inconsistent with an actual 3-factor Box-Behnken design** (which has no "axial points" or "alpha" — those are Central Composite Design, CCD, terms) and doesn't match its own Table 1, which shows only **15 runs total** (matching a standard 3-factor BBD: 12 edge-midpoints + 3 centre-point replicates). This looks like boilerplate CCD language copied into a BBD paper by mistake. **Do not reproduce this description in your own methodology chapter** — describe your own BBD using the correct BBD terminology (edge-midpoints + centre-point replicates), and you may cite this paper as an example of why precise DOE terminology matters.
